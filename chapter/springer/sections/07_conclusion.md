## 7 Conclusion

Importing a sepsis early-warning model is a deployment decision with consequences for patients, and it can be made with a stated statistical guarantee. CAB considers the options available to a receiving hospital in order of increasing intervention: keeping the imported model, moving its alert threshold, and adapting it to local data. It deploys the first configuration whose patient-level sensitivity and false-alert rate are certified by exact binomial tests against requirements fixed in advance, and blocks the model otherwise. Under the assumptions of Section 3.5, chiefly that the labelled patients are a random sample of the receiving hospital's patients and that their labels are correct, the probability of deploying a configuration that violates either requirement is at most a chosen level α, without requiring the developing and receiving hospitals to have the same patient distribution.

In retrospective experiments on two US hospitals, imported models violated the receiving hospitals' requirements in ways that depended on the direction of transfer. The usual approaches, from deploying the model as supplied to point-estimate validation and classical updating, frequently deployed configurations that violated the requirements, including many that appeared acceptable on the hospital's own labelled patients. CAB's observed unsafe-deployment rates were consistent with its bound in every setting examined. Its cost is the labelled data required for certification: in the evaluated settings, a few hundred labelled patients generally led to blocking, while certifying an adapted model in the main transfer required several thousand.

These results support three practical steps for a hospital considering an external model: decide in advance what care will continue if the model is blocked; fix the sensitivity target and the tolerated increase in false alerts before labelling; and plan the number of labelled patients, particularly septic patients, before starting. The guarantee concerns the deployment decision. It does not establish clinical benefit, and any deployed configuration still requires prospective evaluation and monitoring after deployment.
